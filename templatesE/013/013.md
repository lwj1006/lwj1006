# Photoset 013  -  Blue Hydrangea Glasshouse Portrait

**Purpose:** ultra-detailed visual breakdown for prompt construction.  
**Set identity:** pale-blue floral dress, twin-braid hair styling, hydrangea-and-greenery room, glass bowl prop, translucent curtains, high-key botanical daylight, soft romantic indoor garden atmosphere.  
**Recommended generation direction:** use the reference photos as **composition, wardrobe, lighting, and atmosphere references**, not as an identity copy. Keep the final subject as a fictional adult character or anime character. Avoid copying watermarks, platform IDs, logos, or any real-person identity.

---

## 0. Global Photoset DNA

### Core visual concept
This set is built around a bright, soft, botanical indoor portrait environment. The room feels like a small glasshouse, window garden, or florist-styled sunroom rather than a normal bedroom or studio. The visual language combines pale blue florals, fresh green leaves, sheer curtains, reflective glass, and delicate lace textures. The palette is clean and airy: icy blue, hydrangea blue, porcelain white, pale mint, leaf green, translucent aqua, and soft skin tones. The overall emotional tone is dreamy, quiet, fragrant, and slightly fairytale-like.

The model is styled as a delicate botanical heroine surrounded by flowers and glass. The repeated flower motifs in the hair, dress, table arrangement, and background plants make the subject feel integrated into the environment. The scene does not rely on heavy props or dramatic architecture; the strength comes from layered foreground foliage, soft curtain diffusion, white-blue floral massing, and bright sunlight passing through the room.

### Shared wardrobe system
The outfit is a pale-blue, lace-trimmed, floral fantasy dress with a fitted bodice and airy skirt. The bodice has thin shoulder straps, a sweetheart or deep V-shaped neckline, lace edging, small decorative dots or bead-like texture, and corset-style front lacing. The lacing creates a handcrafted romantic detail at the center front without making the garment look heavy. The skirt has a short layered construction with translucent tulle or organza overlays, giving a floating, cloudlike silhouette. The fabric reads as pale blue with white lace and subtle shimmer.

The styling is very light and floral rather than urban. The dress should not look like a hard structured evening gown; it should look soft, sheer, floral, and garden-themed. Important details are: pale blue tone, lace trim, tiny dot texture, delicate ribbon lacing, airy tulle skirt, botanical embellishment, and a faint sparkle under strong daylight.

### Shared hair and accessory system
The hairstyle is dark brown to black, center-parted or softly parted, styled into two thick low braids that fall forward over the chest and shoulders. The braids are decorated with pale-blue flower clips, tiny blue petal stickers, lace ribbons, and small translucent ornaments. The braid styling is essential: it creates the fairytale-garden identity of the set and visually connects the subject to the hydrangeas around her.

Hair should have soft flyaways and loose face-framing strands, not a perfectly polished salon finish. The hair volume is romantic and slightly messy in a controlled way. Flower decorations should appear naturally distributed along the braids and around the temple area, not as a single heavy hair crown.

### Shared environment system
The room is a window-lit botanical corner. It contains white-framed windows, gauzy translucent curtains, hanging vines, fresh green leaves, pale-blue hydrangea clusters, white flowers, and a clear round glass bowl. The background has multiple layers: window frame, curtain veil, green plants, hydrangeas, and occasional hanging flowers. The foreground often contains blurred leaves crossing the frame, creating depth and a sense of looking through a garden.

The floor or tabletop is covered with pale blue and white translucent fabric. This fabric should feel like tulle, organza, or gauze, and it visually extends the dress into the room. The glass bowl is a key prop: it catches light, bends reflections, and adds an aquarium-like or terrarium-like softness. It should be clear, round, reflective, and placed near the lower-left or center-left region depending on composition.

### Shared lighting language
The lighting is high-key daylight, filtered through white curtains and plants. It is bright but soft, with occasional overexposed patches where sunlight hits skin, fabric, or glass. The set relies heavily on bloom, haze, low contrast, and gentle backscatter. Highlights are milky and luminous. Shadows are shallow, cool, and soft, often tinted blue-green by curtains and plants.

The most important lighting effects are: luminous window glow, soft overexposure on white curtains, dappled leaf shadows, glossy highlights on lips and eyes, reflective highlights on the glass bowl, and bloom around pale-blue flowers. The lighting should feel fresh and humid, like morning light in a greenhouse.

### Shared composition language
The photos use a close to medium portrait distance, often with the model surrounded by plants. The frame is dense but not chaotic: leaves and flowers create a natural frame, while the face remains the visual anchor. The camera is usually at eye level or slightly above/forward, with a gentle portrait lens compression. Depth of field is shallow to moderate: the face stays clear, foreground leaves are softly blurred, and background curtains dissolve into light.

The composition should maintain a layered botanical frame: foreground leaves, subject face and dress, glass bowl or hydrangea cluster, and window curtain background. Avoid empty white studio space; this set needs foliage, hydrangeas, gauze, and soft sunlight in every frame.
